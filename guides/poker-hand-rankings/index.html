Poker Hand Rankings for Beginners

Someone bets, two players turn their cards over, and the table goes quiet for a second too long. One new player summed it up in a post titled "Help explaining who wins" — "Can anyone tell me who wins with this hand?" — and got 34 comments (r/poker, 2026-09-01).

Here's the order, with the mistakes that cause most of the arguing.

The order from highest to lowest

1Royal flushA, K, Q, J, 10, all the same suit
2Straight flushFive cards in a row, all the same suit — e.g. 5-6-7-8-9 of hearts
3Four of a kindAll four cards of one rank — e.g. four 9s
4Full houseThree of a kind plus a pair — e.g. three 8s and two 4s
5FlushFive cards of the same suit, not in a row
6StraightFive cards in a row, mixed suits — e.g. 4-5-6-7-8
7Three of a kindThree cards of one rank plus two unmatched cards
8Two pairTwo different pairs plus one unmatched card
9One pairTwo cards of one rank plus three unmatched cards
10High cardNo pair, no sequence, no matching suits — the highest single card plays

What each hand actually looks like

Rankings make more sense next to real examples:

  • Full house vs. flush. 8♣ 8♦ 8♥ 4♠ 4♣ is a full house. 2♠ 6♠ 9♠ J♠ K♠ is a flush. The full house wins — three of a kind plus a pair beats five cards of one suit.
  • Straight vs. three of a kind. 5♦ 6♣ 7♥ 8♠ 9♦ is a straight. Q♣ Q♦ Q♥ 3♠ 7♣ is three of a kind. The straight wins.
  • Two pair vs. three of a kind. K♠ K♦ 7♣ 7♥ 2♠ is two pair. 9♣ 9♦ 9♥ 5♠ 2♣ is three of a kind. Three of a kind wins — it's a step above two pair, even though two pair uses two matching ranks.
  • Ace in a straight. An ace can be the highest card (10-J-Q-K-A) or the lowest (A-2-3-4-5), but never both in the same straight. Q-K-A-2-3 isn't a straight.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Flush mixed up with straight. A flush means all five cards share a suit, in any order. A straight means five cards in a row, in any suit. People at a casual table often say "flush" when they mean "straight" and vice versa.
  • Two pair mistaken for the stronger hand. Two pair feels big because it's two matches, but three of a kind, a straight, a flush, and a full house all beat it.
  • Forgetting suits don't break ties. Hearts never beat spades: all four suits are equal. If two players both have a flush, compare the highest card in each; if those match, compare the next card, and so on down to the fifth. If all five cards match, the pot is split.
  • Not checking the board. In Texas Hold'em, community cards count too. A player who only looks at their own two cards can miss that the board already makes a straight everyone shares — the pot is split unless someone has a better hand, such as a higher straight or a flush.

Quick answers

Does a flush beat a full house?
No. A full house always beats a flush.

Is three of a kind better than two pair?
Yes. Three of a kind beats two pair.

What breaks a tie between two straights?
The straight with the higher top card wins. 5-6-7-8-9 loses to 6-7-8-9-10, and A-2-3-4-5 is the lowest straight of all, with the 5 as its top card. If the top cards match, the pot is split.

What if nobody has a pair?
The hands are compared card by card from the highest down, and the first card that differs decides it. That's a "high card" win, the lowest hand on the list but still a hand.

What's a kicker?
When two players make the same hand, the other cards in their best five break the tie. Say both have a pair of kings: the next highest card decides, and that card is the kicker. K-K-A-7-4 beats K-K-Q-J-9. If the kickers match too, go to the next card. Each player uses their best five cards out of seven — their own two plus the five on the board — so only those five count.

When is the pot split?
When the best five cards of two or more players match rank for rank; suits don't count. A common case is when the board plays: the five shared cards make a better hand than anyone can build with their own two, so everyone still in the hand has the same five cards and shares the pot.
