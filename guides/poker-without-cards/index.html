Forgot Your Deck of Cards?

Everyone's at the table, and the deck isn't. A deck that lives in a drawer at home doesn't always make it into a suitcase. The closest example on r/poker is a missing set of chips: a traveler wrote "On a trip with a lot of poker players but no chips" and asked for an app that tracks chips on everyone's phones.

Here's what you can still do.

When this happens

A few typical cases: a trip or a rental where nobody packed a deck, a spontaneous game night that wasn't planned far enough ahead to buy or find one, or a deck that's simply gone missing — lost, left somewhere, or too worn to deal with (a torn or bent card is recognizable from the back).

What people usually try, and why it's awkward

  • A homemade deck. Paper or cardboard cut into 52 pieces and labeled by hand. It takes real time to make, the pieces are easy to tell apart by wear once you've handled them a few times, and nobody's checking every edge for marks.
  • Photos of cards on a phone. Someone suggests just looking at pictures of a deck on a screen. It falls apart immediately — everyone can see the same screen, there's no way to deal privately, and nothing is actually shuffled or random.
  • A borrowed deck. The obvious fix if a neighbor, host, or hotel front desk has one. It solves the problem completely if someone nearby happens to own cards.

Let phones deal instead

Poker Dealer is a free app that deals straight to each player's own phone. Each player sees their own two cards privately on their own screen, exactly like holding real cards face-down, and the shared cards show up for everyone when it's time to reveal them.

Setting up a table:

  1. The host opens the app and creates a room.
  2. The host shares a code, a link, or a QR code with everyone at the table.
  3. Each player opens it on their own phone and joins — no app download needed. A friend without Telegram opens it in a regular browser instead and types a name.
  4. The host taps to deal, and the app runs the turn order from there.

You've got a full deck again, up to 6 players at one table, as long as everyone has a phone and a signal. The app doesn't count chips or know the size of any bet, so you'll still need real chips, or whatever you'd normally use to track a bet size.

If chips are missing too

The traveler from the thread above got one reply worth remembering: "you can always get creative with chips. Sugar/salt sachets, pieces of paper, bottlecaps, pennies." Anything small, countable, and uniform works as a stand-in stack.
